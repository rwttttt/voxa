Project overview

About VOXA

VOXA is a gesture-to-speech project built for ICT Expo 2026, designed to give people who can't speak an easy, low-friction way to communicate. Its primary mode reads hand shapes through any camera and speaks them aloud, right in the browser — no hardware, no install. A second, wearable mode reads real wrist motion over USB for a hands-free, camera-free alternative, using the same trained model as the desktop app.

Who needs something like this

Millions of people find spoken words hard, unreliable, or temporarily unavailable — after a stroke, with a progressive condition, or simply because speech was never their most consistent channel. VOXA is a small ICT Expo project, not a clinical device, but it sits in that same space: giving a gesture a voice when a voice is hard to give.

Stroke & aphasia survivors
About 38% of stroke patients develop aphasia at onset, and roughly a quarter still have it three months on — gesture can stand in while speech is being relearned.
ALS & progressive motor conditions
As conditions like ALS make speech physically harder to produce over time, a small, reliable set of hand or wrist gestures can keep basic communication going longer.
Nonverbal & minimally-verbal individuals
Including some autistic people, some with severe apraxia, or after brain injury — for whom gesture is often more consistent than spoken words.
Temporary & situational voice loss
Laryngitis, post-surgical recovery, vocal cord strain, or just a loud environment where speaking out loud isn't practical.
VOXA doesn't replace speech therapy or clinical AAC (augmentative and alternative communication) devices — it's a proof of concept built for ICT Expo 2026, showing that gesture-to-speech can run on everyday hardware, in a browser, with no install.

At a glance

Primary mode
Camera-based hand gestures, powered by MediaPipe, runs entirely in your browser.
Secondary mode
Wrist sensor (MPU6050) over Web Serial, demoed with the physical wearable.
Gestures
5 built-in shapes, one hand each — engineered so every gesture is at least two fingers apart from the others, minimizing misreads.
Output
Built-in browser text-to-speech — no server calls, works fully offline once loaded.

How it works

1
Sensor detects motion
A camera tracks your hand, or the wristband streams live wrist motion over USB.
2

A trained classifier matches the shape or motion to a known gesture.
3
